## Keep meaning while reducing reading effort

For a dense or qualified passage, first note its material claims: who does what to whom, under which conditions, with what limits or uncertainty. Track the actor for each action separately; naming someone as a viewer does not also name them as the approver. For a feature limitation, make that feature the grammatical subject so the limitation cannot attach to a person using it.

Rewrite the relationship that causes a second reading. A connective, an explicit actor, or a few extra words can make a compressed phrase easier to follow. Keep established terms and deliberate voice choices that the audience understands. Use this as a local repair, including in Chinese, rather than applying a grammar ban or a sentence-length target to the whole page.

Compare the final copy with those source claims, including after the scrub. Preserve actors, quantities and units, negation, eligibility, timing, comparisons, uncertainty, and exceptions. Preserve the direction of conditions: "if A, then B" does not establish "B only if A." Restore a missing restriction or actor from the source; leave unknowns unknown. Each instruction also needs support from the task or source; add a next action only when that support exists. Done means every material claim remains recoverable, and no new claim or stronger promise entered through the edit.
